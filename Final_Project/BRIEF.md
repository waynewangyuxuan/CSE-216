# Project Brief for Agents

Read this first, then `PACING.md`. Source: the student's submitted proposal and Milestone 1. The original files, screening data and pilot cases were lost, so numbers below are as reported and unconfirmed.

**Project:** "Offloading or Surrendering? Mapping Where AI Assistance Substitutes for Thinking in Research Writing." Solo project, CSE 216 / COGS 230, Fall 2026, instructor Imani Munyaka. The course is about practicing research; novelty is not required.

## Research questions (verbatim)

- RQ1 (Boundary): Across the activities of research writing, which observable patterns of AI use are associated with supporting the writer's thinking (offloading), and which with substituting for it (surrender)?
- RQ2 (Awareness): Which of these substitutions are likely to be visible to the writer, and which are not?
- RQ3 (Signals): What observable features of human-AI writing interactions could indicate substitution and serve as a basis for in-situ feedback?

Success criterion (verbatim): "Progress means a framework that assigns real interactions to these categories with acceptable agreement, and that distinguishes cases existing frameworks cannot."

## Limits promised in the proposal

- No user studies or interviews. RQ2 is analytic only.
- No working system; design concepts stay at mockup level.
- No measurement of skill change.
- The framework describes risk patterns, not deficits in any individual, and codes behavior, not cognition.
- Report model, prompt and settings for any LLM coding.
- Biggest risk: too few genuine research-writing conversations in WildChat. Fallback: a systematization of knowledge (SoK) testing the framework against cases in published studies.

## Reported data, as submitted in Milestone 1 (unconfirmed)

- NIRVANA: M1 says 347 distinct request windows "from a dataset of 77 students", with chat and editor actions. Rebuilt: 347 windows from the 71 students who used ChatGPT. A 30-minute argumentative essay, not research writing.
- WildChat: 2,000 records screened gave 42 clear and 40 possible research-writing candidates, pending human verification. The proposal's target of 100 was not reached. No verified student identities or editor actions.
- ScholaWrite: keystroke logs with 15 intention labels; no AI interaction.
- Pilot: 6 real windows and 16 synthetic examples. Lost.

## Milestone 2 requires

A systematic comparison with existing frameworks (Risko & Gilbert, Shaw & Nave, PAUSE, Maier) identifying cases each cannot distinguish; a review of related work; a codebook applied to 50–100 sequences; agreement statistics and analyzed disagreements; a revised framework. Rubric values honest reporting of problems and adaptations.

## Professor's feedback after the Milestone 1 meeting

- Approves the direction.
- Asked for a reproducible paper-selection process: which Google Scholar keywords, which results, and which LLM prompt screened them.
- Asked how offloading can be judged in a given scene. The working answer is that logs show behavior associated with risk, not certainty about thought.
- Suggested reading on "conceptual analysis", exact referent unknown. Candidates: Jabareen 2009 (doi:10.1177/160940690900800406), Hornbæk & Oulasvirta 2017 (doi:10.1145/3025453.3025765). Neither is confirmed as what the professor meant.
- Earlier written comment: scale back if the group is small.
