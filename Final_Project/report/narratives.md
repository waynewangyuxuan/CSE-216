## 1. NIRVANA e70: from polishing own text to taking AI paragraphs

**Summary.** OFF OFF SL OFF OFF SL SL: the writer first hands over their own paragraphs for polishing (D1), then keeps writing their own body text, and ends by pasting AI-written extension and conclusion text unchanged (D4).

**Phase 1, polishing own drafts (t01-t02).** In NIRVANA-e70-t01 the writer, who had drafted an introduction in the editor, asked the AI to improve it, pasted the polished version unchanged within seconds, and then spent about four minutes typing a first body paragraph of their own. In NIRVANA-e70-t02 they sent that paragraph for the same treatment and again pasted the result unchanged. Codes: F5, D1, V0, U1. Rule R13 reads both as OFFLOADING (moderate): the claims are the writer's, the AI supplied language.

**Phase 2, a requested rework set aside (t03-t04).** In NIRVANA-e70-t03 the writer asked the AI to rework the paragraph around one of the assignment's perspectives. The short reply never reached the editor; the writer instead sent a new request. Codes: F4, D2, V0, U4; R16, SURRENDER_LEANING (weak), on request type alone. In NIRVANA-e70-t04 they asked for the paragraph to present the bleak perspective while noting they disagreed with it. They copied only the AI's closing sentence, folded a reworded version of it into their paragraph, and then typed a new paragraph of their own from the efficiency perspective. Codes: D3, U3; R08, OFFLOADING (moderate).

**Phase 3, AI text carried forward (t05-t07).** NIRVANA-e70-t05 repeats the polish-and-paste pattern on that new paragraph (D1, U1; R13, OFFLOADING, moderate). In NIRVANA-e70-t06 the writer asked how to lengthen the paragraph; the AI answered with a ready-made extension, which was appended unchanged. In NIRVANA-e70-t07 the writer pasted the whole essay into chat, asked for a conclusion, and pasted it unchanged; the session ends there. Both: F4, D4, V0, U1, prior own work visible; R11, SURRENDER_LEANING (moderate). R11 rather than the strong R09 because the writer's own draft was visible.

**What it shows.** Support turns into substitution where the request shifts from improving the writer's text to supplying missing text (t06): the writer stops supplying the content and the AI starts originating it. The log shows the paste and the absence of edits, but not whether the writer judged the extension and conclusion before pasting. R11's main alternative reading applies: by t06 the essay's argument was the writer's, so the AI may only have voiced points the writer already held. The early phase is also not clean support, since every polished paragraph replaced the writer's wording wholesale; whether that matters depends on whether language was part of the learning goal (R13's alternative).

## 2. NIRVANA e26: a writer who mostly keeps the pen

**Summary.** OFF SL OFF AUG IND AUG OFF AUG AUG AUG NEU: one early D4 on an outline, then sustained own composition with the AI used mainly as a critic, and a late D4 that accepts the AI's grade.

**Planning (t01-t02).** In NIRVANA-e26-t01 the writer pasted the assignment and their own brainstorm notes and asked for help building the essay; the AI returned a five-part outline. The writer pushed back on one section, saying their own point behind it was underdeveloped. Codes: F4, D3, V3, U4; R04, OFFLOADING (moderate). In NIRVANA-e26-t02 the AI dropped that section; the writer pasted the revised outline into the editor unchanged, then typed an introduction of their own. Codes: F3, D4, V0, U1; R11, SURRENDER_LEANING (moderate).

**Drafting with critique (t03-t05).** In NIRVANA-e26-t03 the writer asked for a strict grader's critique of their introduction, pasted the AI's rewrite, then added several sentences of their own that set out the perspectives and their own stance (D3, U3; R08, OFFLOADING, moderate). In NIRVANA-e26-t04 they asked for feedback again and, rather than pasting the new rewrite, said it had lost the personal feel of their version (F6, D2, V1, U4; R18, AUGMENTING, moderate). In NIRVANA-e26-t05 the AI offered a more personal rewrite. The writer did not use it: they kept their own introduction and spent about twelve minutes typing the body paragraphs and a closing (F5, D2, U7; R99, INDETERMINATE, because no rule covers this combination).

**Review loop (t06-t11).** NIRVANA-e26-t06, -t08 and -t09 are requests to review the full draft and point to specific problems; nothing the AI wrote entered the essay (D2, F6; R18, AUGMENTING, moderate). In NIRVANA-e26-t07 the writer pasted two grammar-corrected versions of their own sentences plus a small edit of their own (F6, D1, U1; R14, OFFLOADING, weak). In NIRVANA-e26-t10 they asked what grade the draft would earn, were told a mid-range grade, and accepted it and stopped revising (F6, D4, V0, U5; R10, AUGMENTING, weak). NIRVANA-e26-t11 is a thank-you (D0; R02, NEUTRAL_MECHANICAL).

**What it shows.** The single surrender-leaning window (t02) is a structural scaffold, and the writer composed every body paragraph themselves; R11's main alternative reading, that the writer already held the ideas, is strong here. The late D4 (t10) is read as augmenting, but R10's alternative is that accepting the AI's verdict without checking is itself adopting the AI's judgment as one's own; it ended the revision. Reading the text, NIRVANA-e26-t02 looks more like D1 than D4 (the outline's content came from the writer's notes and the assignment, and the cut was the writer's), and NIRVANA-e26-t05 looks more like AUGMENTING than INDETERMINATE (AI rewrite set aside, extended own composition); the latter is a rule gap, not a code error.

**What it shows.** In WildChat, "surrender-leaning" here means only that the user asked the AI to write thesis text and kept asking for more; the log never shows whether any of it entered a thesis, was rewritten, or was discarded. The strength is weak because the window matches a source only by what was requested, the lowest grade v1.2 §9.2 allows. Whether the AI's citations exist is also unseen, since AI correctness is not coded. The codes match the text; the weakness is in what a chat log can show, not in the coding.
